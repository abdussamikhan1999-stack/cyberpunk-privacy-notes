# The alt.cyberpunk FAQ — summary

Source: https://archive.is/mkDpa (archive.is blocks automated fetches from
this environment, so this summary was written from a manually-pasted copy of
the page). Original permalink: https://sizeof.cat/post/cyberpunk-faq/ —
Version 5.1, ~15,900 words, maintained continuously since 1993 (originally a
Usenet `alt.cyberpunk` FAQ, now living on the web).

## What it actually is

A community-maintained FAQ with a genuine paper trail: v1 (Andy Hawks, 1993)
→ v2 (Tim Oerting, 1993) → v3 (Erich Schneider, 1996) → v4 (Frank, 1998) →
v4.1-4.2.5 (Shirkahn/Goobs/Iain x/Aurora Slyde/Sourcerer) → v5.0 (anon, ended
with "the spring purge of /cyb/" in 2021) → v5.1 (current). Worth knowing
because it means the document itself is a small case study in exactly the
"community keeps a living document alive across decades" pattern this repo
is built for.

## Core definition

Cyberpunk = "cybernetics" + "punk": stories about a dominating
technologically-enhanced "system" (corporations, governments, religions) and
the marginalized people on its "Edge" who turn its own tools against it —
"the street finds its own uses for things." Visual/tonal template set by
*Blade Runner* + *Neuromancer*: neon-lit urban dystopia, hard-boiled-noir
moral ambiguity, concepts thrown at the reader unexplained. Traces to Gardner
Dozois popularizing the term (picked up "on the street"), Bruce Bethke's 1980
short story "Cyberpunk," and Bruce Sterling's samizdat zine *Cheap Truth* as
the movement's actual organizing document.

## The subculture, not just the genre

Groups the FAQ associates with cyberpunk-as-identity: **hackers**, **crackers**,
**phreaks**, **cypherpunks** (crypto as a tool against "the System" —
PGP/OpenPGP is the concrete artifact here), plus **transhumanists**,
**biohackers**, **extropians**, **cybergoths**. The FAQ's own take: most of
these are means, not identities — claiming "I am a cyberpunk" outright gets
you flamed, per the newsgroup's own culture.

## How to actually get into it (their reading order, condensed)

1. *Cheap Truth* (the zine that started the Movement).
2. William Gibson: *Burning Chrome* → the Sprawl trilogy (*Neuromancer*,
   *Count Zero*, *Mona Lisa Overdrive*).
3. Bruce Sterling and the other original Movement authors (Shirley, Rucker).
4. Proto-cyberpunk for context: Brunner's *The Shockwave Rider* and *Stand on
   Zanzibar*, Bester's *The Stars My Destination*.
5. Manga/anime: *Battle Angel Alita* (*Gunnm*), *Ghost in the Shell*,
   *Yokohama Kaidashi Kikou* (their pick for "stealth cyberpunk," very slow,
   very good).
6. Film: *Blade Runner* (the essential one), Japanese entries like *Tetsuo:
   The Iron Man*.
7. Technical nonfiction to round it out: networking/RF, CRISPR/biotech, plus
   Feynman's *Cargo Cult Science* and basic organizational theory — "cyberpunk
   is much about how society works, or how it fails to work."

## Where the community actually lives now (as of the FAQ's last update)

Mostly dead: Usenet `alt.cyberpunk.*` is silent since ~2013; 4chan's
`/g/cyb` was active 2013–2021 then had an unexplained mass exodus in 2021;
8ch/lainchan.jp/arisuchan.jp are all gone. What's left: **anon.cafe/cyber**
(the FAQ calls it the current hangout, "comfy style"), **wirechan**,
**8kun.top/cyber/** (slow, lots of broken links), and non-imageboard fora
like `Cyberpunk Forums`. This matters for calibrating expectations: several
links in the original `/cyb/erpunk` thread ([LINKS.md](LINKS.md)) point at
what the FAQ itself describes as a mostly-abandoned scene.

## Related genres, if the aesthetic search continues

Steampunk (origin: Gibson & Sterling's *The Difference Engine*), Clockpunk,
Biopunk (CRISPR-driven fiction), Nanopunk, Dieselpunk/Decopunk/Atompunk
(progressively later 20th-century retrofuturism), Postcyberpunk, Comfypunk,
Tech Noir, Solarpunk/Lunarpunk (optimistic counter-reaction, ~2014 onward),
Nowpunk (cyberpunk set roughly in the actual present — Gibson's own later
trilogies, *Mr. Robot*), Stagnationpunk ("where's my jetpack" — the tech-
optimism-didn't-pan-out genre).

## Technology sections worth flagging for [README.md](README.md)'s privacy/security angle

- **PGP** — the FAQ includes Zimmerman's original "why encrypt your email"
  argument (envelopes vs. postcards) practically verbatim; still the cleanest
  one-paragraph case for default encryption that exists.
- **SDR (Software Defined Radio)** — hobbyist-accessible wideband radio work
  that used to be government-only; `rtl-sdr.com` cited as the resource hub.
  Genuinely useful/legal hobby area, distinct from the fiction.
- **Biohacking/brain hacking/nootropics** — covered descriptively (TMS,
  tDCS, EEG, OpenBCI, Neuralink) as an overview of a real (if fringe) DIY
  research community — not an endorsement or how-to.
- **Cyber/electronic warfare, EMP, RMA** — background/history material, not
  actionable, included here only because it's part of what the FAQ covers.

## Bottom line for this repo

This FAQ is the best single map of "cyberpunk as a real, decades-old
community" that the original thread linked to — worth keeping the full
summary rather than just the link, since the live community infrastructure
it documents (fora, zines, imageboards) has substantially rotted away and
the FAQ itself is the more durable artifact.
